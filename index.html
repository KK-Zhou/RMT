Abstract

Modular multi-legged robots can be reconfigured to accommodate diverse tasks and environments, providing greater morphological flexibility than fixed-configuration robots. However, variations in the number and arrangement of legs substantially change the robot dynamics and inter-limb coordination, making efficient and robust locomotion control across different morphologies challenging. This paper proposes a scalable locomotion primitive transfer framework that leverages model-based bipedal priors and residual reinforcement learning for locomotion across different multi-legged morphologies. Dynamically feasible bipedal demonstrations are first generated using a model-based control pipeline that combines contact-force optimization and whole-body control. Then, the demonstrations are used to learn a command-matching adversarial motion prior, yielding a reusable bipedal primitive policy. Finally, the learned bipedal priors are frozen and reused for morphology transfer. Morphology-specific residual policies adapt the primitives to different multi-legged configurations, with permutation symmetry promoting coordinated actions among primitive groups. Simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework provides a unified approach to locomotion transfer across reconfigurable 2N-legged morphologies, while enabling robust locomotion on challenging terrains.

Method

Overview of the proposed framework. (1) In the motion generation stage, dynamically feasible bipedal demonstrations are generated by a model-based control pipeline and collected as bipedal primitive datasets. (2) In the bipedal unit training stage, the demonstrations are used to train a bipedal primitive policy, while a Wasserstein adversarial critic provides style guidance together with task and energy rewards. (3) In the morphology transfer training stage, multi-legged observations and AMP states are decomposed into biped-compatible primitive groups. The frozen bipedal primitive policy generates base primitive actions, the frozen critic provides style rewards, and a trainable residual policy learns full-action-space corrections for the target morphology. Conclusion

In this paper, we propose ALARM, an end-to-end locomotion control framework for legged robots based on imitation learning and reinforcement learning. Through a single training process, it achieves a seamless transition from teacher to student. By introducing NP3O optimization, it effectively and concisely constrains robot behavior, enabling both safe and robust locomotion on complex terrains using only proprioception. Additionally, ALARM facilitates direct transfer from simulation to the real world, featuring low computational resource consumption and fast inference speed. Through comparative and ablation experiments, we demonstrate that our method surpasses current state-of-the-art reinforcement learning controllers in terms of training efficiency and locomotion performance. Our policy has been deployed on multiple quadrupedal robots, showcasing strong adaptability and resilience in complex environments.
